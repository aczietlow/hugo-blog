Currently just notes

529 accounts

named after section 529 in tax code
Notes
- Tax advantaged 
- named after section 529 in irs tax code
- originally meant to help pay for college 
- use has expanded over time
  - college tuition
  - accrediation programs
  - appreticeship programes
  - student loans 


two types of 529 plans 

- college savings plan
- prepaid tuition
  - lock in tuition rates today

- administered by each state
  - rules will differ slightly from state to state

- college savings program
  - provides preselected funds or target date funds
  - no annual contribution limit
    - instead most 529 accounts have a life time contribution limit varied state by state

taxes
- withdrawls used for qualifying expense are excempt from state and federal taxes
- withdrawls used for non-qualifying expenses are subject to taxes + 10% penalty 
- contributions are deductible on federal taxes
  - some states offer tax deductions or credits for contributions 

what if your child doesn't go to college 
- 529 accounts can easily be transfered to another family member
  - immediate family members like children, spouse, siblings, parents, first cousin
- they can be used to pay other expense such as appreticeship programs, student loans, or credentaling programs
- money can be withdrawn (incures taxes + 10% penalty)
- as of 2023 upto $35,000 of unspend funds can be rolled into a Roth IRA account.
  - account must be at least 15 years old

who maintains a 529 account
- they are technically a custodial account
